# Methodology

This page explains how the numbers on this site are put together, so you can
check our work.

## What counts as a "documented case"

An abuse case is an incident where a Flock Safety ALPR camera network, or the
data it collected, was reportedly used in a way that raises legal, ethical, or
civil-liberties concerns — including warrantless tracking, stalking, sharing
data across agencies or state lines in violation of local law, or targeting
protesters, immigrants, or people seeking reproductive care. Categories are
listed in the [full database](/database/).

## What it takes for a case to be published

Every entry requires **at least one source URL** from a court filing,
government record, public audit log, or reporting by a named outlet. Entries
without a working source link are rejected automatically in our review
process — this is enforced by an automated check, not just editorial policy.

Where a source names an officer, agency, or official, we state only what
that source states, with attribution (e.g. "according to a 404 Media
investigation…"). We use "alleged" for anything that hasn't been
adjudicated, and we do not embellish, infer, or fill in details the source
doesn't provide.

A `verified` flag on each entry means a maintainer has confirmed the cited
source(s) actually support the summary as written.

## Where the camera map data comes from

Camera locations shown on the [map](/map) come only from
[OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) data — the same
crowdsourced dataset [DeFlock.me](https://www.deflock.me) uses — refreshed
weekly via the Overpass API. We never invent or estimate camera coordinates.

## Counting rules

- **Documented cases of abuse**: one entry per file in the abuse case
  database, regardless of how many sources cover it.
- **Cities that canceled or rejected Flock**: one entry per municipality
  that has publicly ended or voted down a Flock contract, each with its own
  source.
- **Pending lawsuits**: legal cases in the tracker with a status of "filed"
  or "pending" — cases marked decided, dismissed, or settled aren't counted
  here, though they remain visible in the [legal tracker](/legal/).

## How to submit a case

See the [submit a case](/database/submit) page for how to propose a new
entry, either via a GitHub pull request or a plain web form. All submissions
are reviewed for sourcing before publication.
